📋 Executive Summary
Key Findings

This comprehensive analysis compares system performance across two CPU frequency configurations: 3200MHz (baseline) and 1600MHz (50% reduction). The results reveal that CPU frequency has a profound impact on latency-sensitive operations while having minimal effect on bandwidth-limited GPU transfer operations.

🔄 Unexpected Result: PCIe Bandwidth
Contrary to expectations, D2H bandwidth IMPROVED at 1600MHz. GPU 2 and 3 D2H performance increased by 40.5% and 38.9% respectively, suggesting frequency-dependent throttling or power management issues at 3200MHz baseline.
⚠️ CPU Latency Impact
CPU-sensitive operations degraded 50-96% at lower frequency: kernel launch latency (+85%), synchronize overhead (+59%), CPU compute time (+43%). These are directly proportional to frequency reduction.
🔴 Critical Anomaly
Maximum CPU-GPU round-trip latency spiked to 2790.18 µs at 1600MHz, a 3940% increase from 91.79 µs at 3200MHz. This indicates significant variance in low-frequency mode scheduling.

💡 Analysis & Recommendations
🔧 Recommended Actions
  • Thermal Investigation: The improved D2H bandwidth at 1600MHz suggests GPU 2 & 3 may be thermally throttled at 3200MHz. Verify cooling system capacity and thermal paste application.
  • Power Delivery Analysis: Reduced H2D performance for GPU 0 & 3 at 1600MHz could indicate power supply issues. Check power delivery to these GPUs.
  • Frequency Scaling Validation: The critical latency spike at 1600MHz (max roundtrip: 2790 µs) indicates potential CPU scaling bugs. Test intermediate frequencies (2400MHz, 2000MHz) to isolate the issue.
  • P2P Bandwidth Investigation: SendRecv performance remains constrained at both frequencies. Investigate NVLink switch settings or PCIe lane allocation for point-to-point traffic.
  • CPU Affinity & Scheduling: Run CPU frequency scaling tests with CPU affinity pinning to determine if thread migration causes latency spikes.
  • BIOS Settings Review: Check for power management states (C-states, P-states) that may interact with frequency scaling.

🎯 Conclusions
Summary of Findings

CPU frequency reduction to 1600MHz (50% reduction) demonstrates significant and complex impacts on system performance:

  • Latency-Sensitive Operations scale nearly linearly with frequency (50-95% degradation), making low-frequency mode unsuitable for latency-critical applications.
  • Throughput Operations show sublinear scaling (32-43% degradation), indicating memory and I/O become bottlenecks.
  • GPU-to-GPU Communication remains robust and even slightly improves, suggesting reduced host CPU interference.
  • Thermal and Power Issues are evident: GPU 2 & 3 show 38-40% D2H improvement, and GPU 0, 3 show H2D degradation, indicating throttling at baseline frequency.
  • Critical Scheduling Anomalies emerge at 1600MHz: maximum latencies spike 30-1000x, revealing OS-level issues that require urgent investigation.
  • P2P Communication Bottleneck is frequency-independent, suggesting hardware constraints in PCIe or NVLink paths.
⚠️ Important Note

While 1600MHz achieves a better pass rate (81.8% vs 72.7%), this masks underlying anomalies: the maximum latency spikes indicate that certain scenarios will experience catastrophic latency, even if median performance is acceptable. Production systems should NOT rely on pass/fail counts alone—detailed latency analysis is critical.
